## Context

IASO Biotherapeutics is hosting an exploratory visit from Butantan Institute (Brazil). A short welcome presentation is needed — not a repurposed investor deck, but a purpose-built narrative for welcoming an international biomedical partner. The CEO will present in English, 5–8 minutes, 5–6 slides.

Source material exists in the analyst deck (67 pages), but must be distilled and reframed from "convince investors" to "welcome a potential partner."

## Goals / Non-Goals

### Goals

- Design a presentation narrative arc optimized for a partner welcome context
- Extract and reframe the most relevant content from the analyst deck
- Ensure collaboration opportunities are concrete and relevant to Butantan's capabilities
- Provide actionable speaker notes in the CEO's voice

### Non-Goals

- Creating the actual PowerPoint file
- Including detailed clinical data, financial projections, or competitive comparisons
- Covering the full pipeline or all technology platforms in depth
- Preparing a bilingual or Chinese version

## Decisions

### 1. Narrative Arc: "Welcome → Who We Are → Why It Matters → Let's Build Together"

**Decision**: Structure the deck as a hospitality-first narrative, not a capabilities showcase. Open with welcome and shared mission, build through company overview and platform highlights, close with collaboration vision.

**Rationale**: Partner visits succeed when the host demonstrates genuine interest in the visitor's capabilities and shared goals, not just their own strengths. A welcome-first structure signals respect and openness. The CEO's role is to set tone, not deliver a technical briefing.

**Alternatives Considered**:
- Standard corporate overview structure (About → Products → Pipeline → Financials): Rejected — too investor-oriented, misses the partnership framing
- Technology-deep-dive structure: Rejected — 5 minutes is insufficient for technical depth; better to invite curiosity for follow-up discussions

### 2. Brazil-Specific Content Placement

**Decision**: Reference Brazil's role in IASO's global strategy naturally within the global expansion slide, not as a separate "why Butantan" slide.

**Rationale**: Explicitly calling out "why we want to work with you" can feel transactional in a first meeting. Instead, showing that Brazil is already part of IASO's global clinical strategy (bridging studies) demonstrates organic alignment without pressure. The CEO can verbally acknowledge the connection in speaker notes.

**Alternatives Considered**:
- Dedicated "Partnership with Butantan" slide: Rejected — too presumptuous for an exploratory first visit
- No Brazil mention at all: Rejected — misses the opportunity to show strategic alignment

### 3. Content Selection from Analyst Deck

**Decision**: Focus on 4 key themes from the 67-page deck:
1. **Mission and positioning** (Page 10–11): "治愈希望" vision, ATMP innovation pioneer
2. **Core product validation** (Page 27, 29): Eque-cel headline efficacy (ORR 98.9%) as proof of execution, not deep clinical data
3. **Platform breadth** (Page 12): 5 integrated platforms, end-to-end capability — positions IASO as a technology partner, not just a product company
4. **Global strategy with Brazil** (Page 19–20, 23): "Products out, patients in" strategy, Brazil as bridging study target

**Rationale**: These 4 themes answer the questions a visiting partner would have: "Who are you?", "Can you deliver?", "What makes you different?", "How do we fit?"

### 4. Visual Strategy

**Decision**: Suggest high-impact visuals that are easy to prepare overnight:
- Team/facility photos (available internally)
- Pipeline graphic (simplified from deck Page 14)
- Global map with Brazil highlighted (adapted from deck Page 23)
- Platform diagram (simplified from deck Page 12)

**Rationale**: Visuals should support conversation, not replace it. Simple, clean visuals with minimal text work best for a short welcome presentation.

## Presentation Structure

### Slide Flow

```
Slide 1: Welcome & Shared Vision          (~1 min)
Slide 2: IASO at a Glance                 (~1.5 min)
Slide 3: Proven Innovation — Eque-cel     (~1.5 min)
Slide 4: Integrated Technology Platforms   (~1 min)
Slide 5: Global Vision — Products Out,    (~1.5 min)
          Patients In
Slide 6: Looking Forward Together          (~1 min)
                                    Total: ~7.5 min
```

### Narrative Logic

The 6-slide structure follows a **hospitality-to-vision arc**:

1. **Open with warmth** (Slide 1): Acknowledge the guest, establish shared purpose. This sets the emotional tone — "we are honored to host you."
2. **Establish credibility quickly** (Slides 2–3): Who we are and what we've achieved. The CEO earns the right to discuss collaboration by demonstrating execution capability.
3. **Show depth without drowning** (Slide 4): Platform breadth signals that IASO is a technology partner, not a one-product company. This is where Butantan's R&D expertise finds natural connection points.
4. **Reveal strategic alignment** (Slide 5): The global strategy slide naturally positions Brazil, creating an "aha moment" — "they're already thinking about our region."
5. **Close with invitation** (Slide 6): End with a forward-looking, open-ended invitation. Not "here's what we want from you" but "let's explore what we can build together."

This structure works because it mirrors how trust is built in a first meeting: warmth → credibility → shared interests → invitation.

## Risks / Trade-offs

### Risk: Oversimplification

**Risk**: Condensing a 67-page deck into 6 slides may lose nuance that Butantan's scientific team expects.

**Mitigation**:
- Position the deck explicitly as a "conversation starter" — deeper technical discussions to follow
- Have backup slides or the full analyst deck available for Q&A
- Speaker notes guide the CEO to invite specific follow-up topics

### Trade-off: Breadth vs. Depth

**Decision**: Prioritize breadth (mission, product, platforms, global strategy) over depth (clinical data, manufacturing details).

**Impact**: Butantan's scientists may want more detail on specific platforms. The CEO should be prepared to pivot to SMEs (CSO, CMO) for technical follow-up during discussion.
